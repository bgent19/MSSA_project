### Finding 1: the first scaffold had Identity and EF Core; we threw it away

Brett's initial scaffold was created with Individual Accounts, which pulled in
`Microsoft.AspNetCore.Identity.EntityFrameworkCore`, `EntityFrameworkCore.SqlServer`, EF
Design/Tools, a `Data/` folder with `ApplicationDbContext` + `ApplicationUser` + migrations,
a `Components/Account/` tree, and a LocalDB connection string the app **throws on startup
without**. That directly contradicts two constraints already settled on the map ("No
authentication in this sprint", "No EF Core or real database in this sprint").

Re-scaffolded clean with `-au None`. Cost: about two minutes, and nothing was lost — there
were no commits and no code of Brett's, only template output. Gain: `Program.cs` went from
~60 lines to **27**, all of them explicable, and the app now boots with zero external setup.
The rubric grades branching, loops, methods, classes, OOP design and data structures;
scaffolded auth demonstrates none of them while consuming budget and adding a demo-day
dependency on LocalDB being healthy.

**Rule for sprint day: if a `dotnet new` invocation offers auth, the answer is `-au None`.**

### Finding 2 (the important one): interactivity is per-page, not app-wide

Hot reload appeared broken — Brett edited `Home.razor`, saved, and the browser did not
change until they navigated away and back. Hot reload was working the whole time. The cause
is render mode:

- `Components/Pages/Home.razor` has **no** `@rendermode` → statically server-rendered, no
  SignalR circuit, so the server has no open connection to push a re-render down. The
  browser was showing HTML handed to it earlier; navigating caused a fresh request.
- `Components/Pages/Counter.razor` has `@rendermode InteractiveServer` → has a circuit.
  Verified: editing its markup while sitting on the page updates live, no navigation.

**This is the trap to carry into the sprint.** `-int Server` gives *per-page* interactivity.
Any page with a button, a form, or an `@onclick` needs `@rendermode InteractiveServer` at
the top. Omit it and the failure mode is the worst kind: the page renders perfectly, looks
entirely correct, and clicks silently do nothing — no error, no exception, no breakpoint hit.
Easily 45 minutes lost. Recorded as a constraint on
[Prototype the screens and the demo click path](07-prototype-screens.md).
